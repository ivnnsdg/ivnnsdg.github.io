Popsicle-Stick Truss Bridge

This project was completed as part of a Statics course at Shippensburg University. The objective was to design and construct a popsicle-stick truss bridge spanning four stick lengths, with a clear roadway between the side trusses and the ability to support a load applied at midspan. The primary design goal was to maximize the bridge's load-to-weight ratio, achieving the greatest possible load-bearing capacity while minimizing the structure's own weight.

I explored numerous truss configurations using a bridge simulation tool, evaluating force distribution, member lengths, tension and compression, and the contribution of individual elements to the overall weight. Through repeated simulations and design iterations, I identified structural weaknesses and worked to balance structural efficiency with material usage. I further refined the structure by cutting and modifying individual members, combining wooden sticks in different orientations, and reinforcing pinned joints. The final design was built and evaluated through physical load testing.
